AI systems now find software vulnerabilities at scale. Using the full CVE List V5 (405,375 records, 12 Nov 2026), the CISA KEV catalogue (1,779 entries), 33 curated financial-sector incidents and a group-level framework for Thailand, we study what this means for banks and central banks. CVE publications in Jan-Oct 2026 were 2.27x the 2025 level, led by commercial vendors (Chrome x33, Oracle x13, Microsoft x4). At least 1,598 CVEs credit an AI system or AI-native firm; the monthly count doubles about every four months, and AI-credited bugs are as severe as human-credited ones. Exploitation has grown more slowly than discovery: within-180-day exploitation fell from 3.9 to 2.6 per 1,000 new CVEs, and only 15 AI-credited CVEs entered KEV, mostly as n-days. A KEV-based zero-day proxy matches industry counts for 2022-2024 within 7%. Incidents show zero-days reach banks through shared commercial products (file transfer, mail, perimeter devices), not core banking. A Monte Carlo model of a Thai-like system calibrated to KEV rates finds that tripling AI offensive capacity raises the annual probability of a systemic payment disruption about fivefold (4.0-6.8x), AI-enabled defence removes 50-75% of the increase, and vendor concentration adds 36-64%. We draw ten implications for central banks.
